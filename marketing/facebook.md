I cloned my voice, wrote a 30-minute Bible study, and published it as an audiobook and a four-part series. The whole narration cost me four cents.

Not four dollars. Four cents.

Here is how, because the method matters more than the numbers.

The voice: two minutes of me reading Scripture, cloned twice, once on ElevenLabs and once with a free open-source studio running on my own machine.

The split: English narration went to the free local clone. Korean narration, and the two opening moments where Scripture is quoted plainly, went to the paid cloud clone. English is 35 of the audiobook's 68 minutes, so most of the book costs nothing.

The count, measured, not guessed:
- All-cloud build of the same book: 17,200 credits, about $2.85.
- Hybrid build: 229 credits, about $0.04.
- Machine time: 76 minutes of my laptop's CPU, while I did something else.

What I did not expect: the free local clone matched my timbre more closely than the paid cloud clone did, and it read 35% too fast until I slowed it to my own speaking rate. Both facts came from measurement, not from listening and guessing.

The tool is called VoiceForge, it is open source, and it runs on Python with no cloud account needed for the free path.

Repo: https://github.com/henrynkoh/VoiceForge

If you narrate anything, sermons, teaching, courses, or a podcast you have been putting off, this is the cheapest path I have found that still sounds like a person.
