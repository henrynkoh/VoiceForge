# Newsletter

**Subject:** 30 minutes of audiobook for 4 cents

**Preheader:** Two clones, one manuscript, and the split that did the work.

---

Hey,

I narrated a 30-minute book in my own cloned voice last week. The bill was four cents.

Here is the split that made that possible, and it is simpler than it sounds.

**The setup.** I cloned my voice twice: once on a cloud service that bills per character, once with a free open-source studio running on my own laptop.

**The split.** English sections, about 35 minutes of audio, went to the free local clone. Korean sections, about 33 minutes, went to the paid one, because the free model I could run on CPU is English-only.

**The result.**

- All cloud: 17,200 credits, about $2.85
- Hybrid: 229 credits, about $0.04
- Same audio quality, same voice, same 30 minutes and 13 seconds

**The surprise.** Measured against my own recordings, the free clone was a closer match to my timbre than the paid clone (0.959 versus 0.815). What it lacked was pacing: it read 35% too fast. Slowing it to my own speaking rate, 13.3 characters per second, closed the gap.

**Two things worth stealing even if you never clone a voice:**

1. Pay for the paragraphs that must be perfect; let the free tool read the rest.
2. Measure the output. The pace problem sounded like a quality problem until it was measured.
