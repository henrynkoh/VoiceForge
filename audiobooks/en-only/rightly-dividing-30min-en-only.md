# Rightly Dividing the Word of Truth

A thirty-minute audiobook for English-speaking listeners.
Narrated in the cloned voice. English teaching, King James Version scripture.

Scripture rule used here: every verse is quoted in the King James Version exactly as it stands. Nothing paraphrased, nothing reworded.

---

## Chapter 1. The Book That Feels Like a Puzzle

Friend, welcome. Find a chair, and bring your Bible, because today we are going to do something simple. We are going to ask one question of the whole Book, and then we are going to let the Book answer it.

Here is the question. Who is this written to?

Not, what does this mean to me. Not, how does this make me feel. Who is this written to. Because once you begin to ask that question honestly, a strange thing happens. The Bible stops feeling like a puzzle with pieces missing, and it starts reading like a letter. A letter written on purpose, to a people, in a time, with a promise.

Most people who give up on Bible reading do not give up because they are lazy. They give up because they are honest. They read a psalm where God promises to make their enemies their footstool, then they read the Sermon on the Mount, then they read Romans, and the three do not sound like they came from the same office. So they close the Book and quietly decide the problem is them.

The problem is not them. The problem is that they were handed a stack of letters and told all of them were addressed to the same person, at the same time, with the same instructions.

Imagine a family where the father writes three letters. To his young son: do not cross the road alone. To his grown daughter: drive carefully on the highway. To his business partner: sign nothing until the lawyer reads it. Every letter is true. Every letter is from the same father. But if you hand all three to the boy and tell him they are all for him, you have not honored the father. You have confused the son.

Have you ever noticed how a single sentence can be true in one letter and dangerous in another. A father writes, spend the money. To the son buying bread, it is provision. To the son buying a boat, it is ruin. You would never say the father contradicted himself, because you understand how a letter works. We simply stopped reading the Bible as a library of letters and started reading it as one page of rules.

That is what has happened to the Bible in many of our hands. Not a book that contradicts itself, but a book with more than one addressee, given to us by people who forgot to check the address.

And here is why it matters. Confusion is not neutral. Confusion produces two bad children. The first is pride, the man who is certain about everything and has stopped listening. The second is despair, the woman who is certain about nothing and has stopped reading. Right division is the cure for both, because it lets the Bible say what it says, to whom it says it, without flinching.

So for the next half hour, we are going to learn to read the address. We will look at one verse that commands it, two audiences that are often confused, two systems of law and grace, two gospels that are not the same gospel, and then a simple way for you to read tomorrow morning.

And let me say this before we begin. This is not a trick, and it is not a hobbyhorse. It is the difference between a Bible that sits on your shelf and a Bible that opens in your hands.

## Chapter 2. What It Means to Rightly Divide

Now to the verse that gives this teaching its name. It is Paul writing to a young man named Timothy, and it is the last letter Paul will ever write.

2 Timothy chapter 2, verse 15. "Study to shew thyself approved unto God, a workman that needeth not to be ashamed, rightly dividing the word of truth."

Look at how much is in that one sentence.

First, "study." The word means be diligent. It is not a suggestion to read more; it is a command to work. God is not impressed by a man who owns a Bible. He is looking for a workman.

Second, "approved." The approval is God's, not the crowd's. There is a kind of Bible teaching that is approved by everybody because it says nothing in particular. Paul is not aiming at that. He is aiming at the approval of the One who gave the Book.

Third, "a workman that needeth not to be ashamed." Shame is the feeling of a job badly done. You know the feeling. You cut a board short, and you do not want anyone to see the joint. Paul says there is a way to handle the word of truth that leaves no joint to hide.

And fourth, "rightly dividing." To divide rightly. The picture is a workman with a line, cutting straight. No wobble. No guesswork. Not hacking the Book into pieces, and not stirring it into a soup where everything tastes the same. Cutting where God cut.

That is the whole idea. God has already put seams in His word. Our job is not to invent them. Our job is to find them and cut along them, so the Book comes apart in the shape God gave it.

Notice also what Paul does not say. He does not say, rightly feel the word of truth. He does not say, rightly enjoy. He says rightly divide, which is a work of the mind and the hands, done slowly, done the same way twice, done by someone willing to be corrected by the text.

And here is the promise attached. A man who does this is not ashamed. Not because he is clever, but because he is faithful. He did not make the Book say what he wanted. He let it say what it says.

So we have our command. Now we need the seams. And the first seam, the biggest one, runs right through the middle of the Bible: two different peoples, with two different callings.

## Chapter 3. Two Peoples, Not One

Here is the seam. God has two purposes running through history, and they are not the same purpose, though they meet in the same Lord.

The first is a nation. Israel, chosen, called out of Egypt, given a land, given a law, given promises about a kingdom on this earth with a king on a throne in Jerusalem. That is not a picture of heaven. That is a kingdom with dirt and borders and a capital city.

The second is a body. A body of people, Jew and Gentile together, with no land, no temple, no priesthood of their own, joined to a risen Christ in heaven, waiting not for a throne on earth but for a Lord to come and take them to be with Him.

Both are true. Both are in your Bible. And they are not the same thing.

Paul calls the second one a mystery. Not a riddle, but a secret that had not been told. Ephesians chapter 3, verse 2. "If ye have heard of the dispensation of the grace of God which is given me to you-ward:"

The word there is dispensation. A household management. God running His household in a particular way for a particular season. Paul says a management of grace was given to him, for you.

Then verse 6 tells us what the secret was. "That the Gentiles should be fellowheirs, and of the same body, and partakers of his promise in Christ by the gospel:"

Fellowheirs. Same body. Partakers of the promise. Gentiles, without becoming Jews, brought into one body with believing Jews, on equal footing, by faith in the gospel.

Now listen carefully, because this is where the confusion is born. If you do not see the two peoples, you will do one of two things. You will either make the church into Israel, and start hunting in the prophets for a throne you were never promised, and a temple you were never told to build. Or you will make Israel into the church, and decide that God is finished with a nation He has not finished with.

Both errors feel pious. Both steal something. The first steals your peace, because you are now responsible for promises made to somebody else. The second steals God's faithfulness, because you have decided He broke His word to a people He swore to.

Right division refuses both. Israel is Israel, and God will keep every promise He made to her, literally, on this earth, in a kingdom that is coming. The body of Christ is the body of Christ, called out during this age of grace, with a heavenly calling, waiting for a Saviour from heaven.

Two peoples. Two callings. One Lord. That is the first seam, and everything else we look at today hangs from it.

## Chapter 4. Law and Grace Are Not a Blender

The second seam is not about people. It is about how God deals with people. And the two ways are so different that mixing them makes a third thing that God never gave.

Romans chapter 6, verse 14. "For sin shall not have dominion over you: for ye are not under the law, but under grace."

Read that as a sentence, not as a slogan. Paul gives a reason. Why will sin not rule over you? Because you are not under law, but under grace. Not because you are strong. Because of where you are standing. The location changed.

Under law, the question is, what must I do. Under grace, the question is, what has been done for me. Under law, my obedience is the ground of my standing. Under grace, my standing is the ground of my obedience. Those are not two flavors of the same religion. They are two different foundations, and only one of them can hold a house.

Now watch what happens when you blend them. A man mixes a little law into grace to keep people serious. So he preaches, you are saved by grace, but if you stumble too often, you were probably never saved. What has he done? He has made the cross a down payment and his own performance the mortgage. And the man in the pew cannot tell whether he is accepted or on probation.

Or a man mixes a little grace into law to keep people comfortable. So he says, God understands, nobody is perfect, just be sincere. What has he done? He has taken the law's standard away and left the sinner without a Saviour, because a man who has not been shown his debt will never love the One who paid it.

Right division does not blend. It says the law is holy, and it did its work, and it showed me I cannot stand before God on my own feet. Then it says grace is grace, and it gave me a standing I could never earn.

And here is the end of the matter, in one verse. Romans 6, verse 23. "For the wages of sin is death; but the gift of God is eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord."

Wages and gift. A wage is earned. A gift is received. You cannot earn a gift, and you cannot receive a wage as though it were charity. Two words, two systems, and a man who confuses them will spend his life trying to pay for something that was already bought.

## Chapter 5. Two Gospels, and Why the Difference Matters

Now the seam that troubles people the most, because it sounds at first like we are saying there are two ways of salvation. We are not. There is one Saviour. But there is more than one announcement, and the announcements are made to different people about different things.

First, the gospel of the kingdom. Matthew chapter 4, verse 17. "From that time Jesus began to preach, and to say, Repent: for the kingdom of heaven is at hand."

Read it in its setting. A Jewish Messiah, standing in Galilee, speaking to Israel, announcing that the kingdom promised by the prophets was now near, and the King was in front of them. The message was, the King is here, so repent and receive Him. That is a kingdom announcement, and it is addressed to a nation.

Then, after the cross, after the rejection, after the risen Christ stops Saul on the road, a different announcement is given, and Paul says it came to him by revelation, not from the other apostles.

1 Corinthians chapter 15, verses 3 and 4. "For I delivered unto you first of all that which I also received, how that Christ died for our sins according to the scriptures; And that he was buried, and that he rose again the third day according to the scriptures:"

Do you see the difference in the content? The kingdom gospel says, the King is at hand, receive Him and the kingdom comes. The grace gospel says, the King died for your sins, was buried, and rose again, and by that finished work you are saved.

Now here is why the distinction is not a technicality. If you take the kingdom message and make it the gospel for this age, you end up telling people to repent to get a kingdom that is not being offered yet, and you end up reading the Sermon on the Mount as a ladder instead of a law for a kingdom that has not arrived. If you take the grace message and lay it over the prophets, you end up embarrassed by promises God fully intends to keep.

And if you blend them, which is what most of us were taught to do, you get a gospel that is partly an announcement and partly a contract, and you can never be sure which part you are standing on.

Paul said it plainly, and we should say it plainly with him. Grace is not a reward for repenting well. Grace is God doing for us, in Christ, what we could not do, and then handing us the receipt with our name on it.

The kingdom is not cancelled. It is postponed, and it is certain. The King will sit on the throne of His father David, and the prophets were not exaggerating. But the throne is not today's business. Today's business is the gospel of the grace of God, and the body being built by it.

## Chapter 6. Questions People Actually Ask

Before we get practical, let us answer the questions that come up every time this teaching is given, because they are honest questions and they deserve more than a slogan.

The first question: does this mean the words of Jesus do not apply to me. No. It means the words of Jesus were spoken to somebody, in a setting, for a reason, and that reason is part of the meaning. When the Lord told a leper, show yourself to the priest and offer the gift Moses commanded, He was operating inside a system that was still running. When He told a rich young ruler to sell everything, He was exposing a heart, not writing a policy for every believer in every age. The red letters are never less than inspired. They are not always addressed to you in the same way. Reading them as history first, and as instruction second, does not diminish them. It lets them say what they meant before we make them say what we want.

The second question: if the kingdom is postponed, what was the point of the Sermon on the Mount. The Sermon describes the righteousness of the kingdom He was offering, and it shows every honest listener that he cannot produce it. That is not a failure of the Sermon. That is the Sermon doing its work. It is the same work the law did. It shuts your mouth and sends you looking for a righteousness you did not manufacture.

The third question: was Paul the only one who taught this. No, but he was the one to whom the mystery was revealed, and he said so repeatedly. Peter, James and John ministered to the circumcision. Paul ministered to the uncircumcision, and he wrote thirteen letters that are the constitution of the body of Christ. When those letters and the earlier ministry seem to pull in different directions, the answer is usually that they are pulling in different directions on purpose, because they are addressed to different people about different things.

The fourth question: what about Israel today. God has not replaced Israel, and God has not forgotten Israel. Paul spends three chapters in Romans arguing that the gifts and calling of God are without repentance. There is a blindness in part upon Israel until the fulness of the Gentiles comes in, and then the Deliverer comes out of Zion. If you read that as poetry about the church, you have taken comfort from a promise and left the promise holder standing outside in the dark.

The fifth question: does this make me a better Christian than someone who has not seen it. Absolutely not, and the moment you think so, you have stopped rightly dividing and started proudly dividing. This is a tool for reading, not a badge for wearing. The man who sees the seam and becomes arrogant has missed the point of the seam.

Those five questions cover most of what people actually ask. And notice that every answer comes back to the same habit: find out who is being spoken to, and when, and what was on the table at that moment.

## Chapter 7. Two Destinies, Not One

Here is a seam that runs underneath the others, and once you see it, the last book of your Bible stops being a horror film and starts being a promise.

God is not taking everyone to the same place. That sentence sounds wrong to some ears, so let us say it more carefully. God is not giving everyone the same destination, and He never said He would. He has a purpose for the earth, and He has a purpose for heaven, and He has said so on purpose, in plain words, more than once.

The earth has a future. Read the prophets and try to make them mean something else, and you will twist every page. A land, a city, a throne, a King, a nation restored, the desert blooming, the nations coming up to worship. That is not a metaphor for a spiritual experience. That is a kingdom, and it is coming to this planet, and the One who will sit on that throne has already been born in Bethlehem and has already paid for it.

Heaven has a people too. A body joined to a risen Christ, seated with Him in heavenly places, blessed with spiritual blessings, waiting for a Saviour who will come to the air and take them to be where He is. No temple, no land, no earthly throne in that calling. A heavenly calling, with a heavenly hope, held by a heavenly Head.

Now put those two side by side and notice how much confusion disappears. When you read the prophets and you are looking for a rapture into heaven, you will feel the text straining. When you read Ephesians and you are looking for a throne in Jerusalem, you will feel the same strain. Neither passage is confused. We are the ones who mixed up the tickets.

And this is not a cold technical point, friend. It is comfort. It means the promises God made to a grieving nation are not absorbed into your church attendance. It means the hope He gave you is not second-hand. It means that when the world burns and the King comes, Israel will have her King, and when the trumpet sounds and the Lord comes for His body, you will have your Saviour. Nothing gets lost in the handoff, because there is no handoff. There are two purposes, and one God who does not forget either one.

Paul prayed that the eyes of our understanding would be enlightened, that we might know the hope of His calling. That prayer is answered in the same way every other prayer is answered: by opening the Book and letting it tell you where you are going, instead of telling the Book where you have decided to go.

## Chapter 8. Where the Confusion Came From

If all of this is in the Book, why did so few of us hear it. That is a fair question, and the answer is not flattering to any of us.

The confusion came in through a door that was left open for a good reason. When the Lord Jesus walked this earth, He ministered to Israel, under the law, announcing the kingdom. Those words are in red letters, and we loved the red letters, and rightly so. But we took the red letters and made them the whole instruction manual for a people who had not yet been told about the cross, the resurrection, the Spirit indwelling them, or the body of Christ.

That is why a believer reads, sell what you have and give to the poor, and feels guilty on Sunday. Not because the command was not real. Because it was a command given inside a kingdom offer to a nation under law, and the man reading it lives after the cross, in the age of grace, with a different calling.

The confusion also came in through impatience. The transition in the book of Acts is not tidy. Israel's leadership rejects the risen Christ, and step by step God turns, and Paul is raised up, and the mystery is revealed. If you flatten that transition, which many teachers have done for the sake of a clean outline, you lose the seam and you inherit the confusion.

And a third door was pride. It is easier to build a system and defend it than to sit with a text that will not fit the system. Every one of us has a favourite verse we use as a hammer. Right division is the discipline of putting the hammer down.

So what do we do with the difficulty. We do not throw away the parts that do not fit. We read them in order. We ask who, and when, and what was being offered, and what was being required. We let the Bible be a story with movements, not a bag of verses with a label on each one.

And we keep our confidence in the Book, not in our outline. The outline is a servant. The Book is the authority.

## Chapter 9. How to Read Tomorrow Morning

So, friend, you have the seam. Here is what to do with it, starting tomorrow, without buying a commentary or learning a system.

First, read a whole letter, not a verse. Pick Ephesians and read it in one sitting, and do not stop to look anything up. You are listening for the shape of the thing, the way you would listen to a friend tell a story.

Second, before you read, ask three questions of the passage you are about to open. Who is speaking. Who is being spoken to. What has already happened in the story when these words are said. Write the answers in the margin. You will be surprised how often those three questions dissolve a difficulty you have carried for years.

Third, mark the two words every time you meet them: Israel, and the body. When you see promises to a nation and a land and a throne, do not steal them, and do not spiritualize them away. When you see the mystery, the one new man, the heavenly calling, do not drag it back down to a kingdom on earth. Let each keep its own address.

Fourth, when you meet a verse you cannot place, do not force it. Put a mark beside it and keep reading. Some verses are settled by the next chapter, and some by the next year. A workman is not a man who has an answer for everything. He is a man who keeps cutting straight.

And fifth, do this with your Bible open and your mouth shut for a while. Right division is not a weapon for winning arguments with other believers. It is a way of standing under the text so that the text can correct you. Paul told Timothy to be a workman who is not ashamed, and then, in the very next verses, he told him to be gentle to those who oppose him. The same chapter holds both commands. Cutting straight and staying kind.

Now let me close where the whole thing ends, because it does not end in a diagram. It ends in a person.

The wages of sin is death, and we earned that. The gift of God is eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord, and we did not earn that, and we never will. If you have never received that gift, then the most important thing in your life is not a study method. It is this. Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ, and thou shalt be saved.

And if you have received it, then friend, you have a Book in your hands that was written on purpose, to a people, in a time, with a promise, and you have been given the workman's job of cutting straight. Not to prove you are right. To show that He is faithful.

And let me leave you with one small discipline that has helped me more than any chart. When you close your Bible in the morning, say back to God in one sentence what you just read, in your own words, and then say whose sentence it was. Not a prayer request. Not a sermon. One sentence, addressed honestly.

If you can do that for a month, you will know more about handling the word of truth than a decade of listening to other men handle it for you.

So tomorrow morning, open it. Ask who. Cut straight. And do not be ashamed.
